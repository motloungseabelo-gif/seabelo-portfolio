Why I build

Every project starts with a reason, not a technology.

I want my work to show judgment, curiosity and an ability to connect software decisions to real people and real outcomes.

01

To reduce friction

I am drawn to problems where information is scattered, decisions are harder than they need to be, or a user journey can be made simpler.

02

To understand systems

I build to learn what sits underneath an interface: data relationships, business rules, state, validation, deployment and the consequences of technical choices.

03

To grow through evidence

Instead of only saying I am learning, I use projects to create evidence of how I think, where I struggled and how I improved the next version.

How I think

I care about the decision behind the feature.

When I work on software, I first ask what the user is trying to accomplish and what would make that path clearer. A polished interface is useful only when it supports the problem being solved.

I also pay attention to trade-offs. A more impressive animation is not automatically better if it makes a page slower. More features are not automatically better if they make the experience harder to understand. Good software is often the result of deciding what deserves to stay simple.

Problem first

What is actually difficult for the user?

Constraint aware

What can be delivered reliably with the time, tools and data available?

Measure the experience

Does it load well, behave reliably, work on mobile and communicate clearly?
